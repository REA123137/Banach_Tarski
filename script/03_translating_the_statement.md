# 03 · Translating the statement: “doubleable”

**02:00 – 03:15** · 187 words · Iasmina 85 / Rym 102
**Purpose** — turn the magic trick into a precise mathematical goal.

**Scenes** `S03Doubleable · S03Disjoint · S03Machine`

---

**IASMINA**  Proving this theorem means proving one thing only: that the unit ball is doubleable.
And being doubleable means precisely this: the ball is a union of disjoint sets, each of which is equidecomposable with the whole ball.

> **ON SCREEN** — L = U ⊔ V,  U ∼ L,  V ∼ L

**RYM**  Those two words I will explain. For now, hold on to the shape of the sentence: two batches that do not overlap, and each one gives back the entire ball.
Start with the easy one. Two sets are disjoint when their intersection is empty.

> **ON SCREEN** — A ∩ B = ∅

**IASMINA**  The intersection of two sets is the set of elements belonging to both at once. Saying it is empty says that no element is in both.
Translated to our ball: every point belongs to one batch, and to exactly one. No point counted twice, no point left out.

> **ANIMATION** — Two overlapping set blobs, the shared region hatched in red: rejected. Then the blobs pull apart, the red vanishes: accepted. A single point wanders and always lights up in one blob only.

**RYM**  Equidecomposable is the heart of the matter, and we are getting there.
*beat* — Which leaves the question that counts: how do we build those batches?
The answer is one idea. We build them out of a sequence of motions. A string of rotations, performed one after another, and those strings are what will sort the points of the ball.
