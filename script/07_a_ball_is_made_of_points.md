# 07 · A ball is not a catalogue: it is made of points

**09:00 – 10:20** · 197 words · Iasmina 107 / Rym 90
**Purpose** — name the central difficulty, then introduce matrices.

**Scenes** `S07WordsVsPoints · S07Magnifier · S07Matrices · S07DialMachine`

---

**IASMINA**  So far we have been sorting words. A word is a string of symbols: easy to file away, you just look at its first letter.
A ball is not like that. A ball is a set of points, and a point carries no label. It has no first letter. It has nothing at all, apart from its position.

> **ANIMATION** — On the left, the neat columns of words. On the right, the ball filled with infinitely many anonymous, identical points.

**RYM**  That is the whole difficulty of the theorem, and it is what took genius in 1924.
We need our letters to act on points. In other words, each letter has to be a genuine rotation of space, one that takes a point and carries it somewhere else.
And for handling rotations there is a tool built for the job: matrices.
A rotation about the vertical axis, through an angle θ, is written like this. It takes the coordinates of a point and hands you back the coordinates of its image.

> **ON SCREEN** — the two matrices A and B.

**IASMINA**  A turns about the vertical axis, B about a horizontal one. The inverses, A⁻¹ and B⁻¹, are the same rotations the other way.
From now on, our letters are these matrices. The word a b becomes the matrix product A B, and it acts on a point by genuinely moving it.

## Animation notes

**The book that becomes a point.** Split screen. On the left, a perfectly legible book spine. On the right, a point of the ball. Bring a magnifier to the book: you read the title. Bring the same magnifier to the point: nothing, it carries no label, and the lens merely enlarges a white dot. — `S07Magnifier`

**Then the matrices arrive as machines** with three input dials and three output dials: feed in (0, 1, 0), the dials spin, the output shows. — `S07DialMachine`; the numbers on the output dials are the real product.
