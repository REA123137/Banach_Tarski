# 05 · The group F₂, and why it must be free

**04:35 – 06:20** · 253 words · Iasmina 100 / Rym 153
**Purpose** — define F₂, then define “free”, and say why it is indispensable.

**Scenes** `S05Catalogue · S05LibraryFlight · S05GroupAndFree · S05RubikNotFree · S05SamePage`

---

**RYM**  The set of all these reduced words has a name. We write it F₂.

> **ON SCREEN** — F₂ = { all reduced words written with a, a⁻¹, b, b⁻¹ }

**RYM**  In plain English: F₂ is the catalogue of every possible sequence of motions. Every single one, including the ones a thousand letters long.
Jorge Luis Borges imagined a library containing every possible book: every possible string of letters, without exception. Almost all of its volumes are gibberish, and yet everything that can be written is in there. He called it the Library of Babel.
Our catalogue is a Library of Babel made of motions. It holds every possible way of turning a ball.

**IASMINA**  We call it a group, simply because there are three things we know how to do with it: chain two words into a third, do nothing at all, and undo what we have done.
*beat* — And now the property that everything depends on. This group has to be free.

> **ON SCREEN** — A group is free when two different words always name two different elements, unless their equality already follows from the group axioms alone.

**RYM**  Let us translate. The only simplifications allowed are the ones in our game: a letter cancels its inverse, and that is all. No other coincidence is permitted.
This is not automatic. It is actually rather rare. On a Rubik's cube, for instance, turning the same face four times brings you right back to where you started.

> **ANIMATION** — The cube face turns four times and the cube is identical again. Underneath, the word a a a a writes itself, then collapses to e.

**IASMINA**  In other words, on a cube the word a a a a equals the empty word even though nothing cancelled. The Rubik's cube group is not free: it has shortcuts.
We want the opposite. We want a catalogue with no shortcuts at all, where two different sequences of motions always leave you in different states.

## Animation notes

**The Library of Babel.** The camera flies through an infinite hexagonal library, Borges style, where every book spine carries a word: ab, aab, ba⁻¹b. We pull one volume at random, open it, and it holds a drawn sequence of rotations. — `S05LibraryFlight`

**And here is how to stage the word “free”.** In a library that is not free, the Rubik's cube one, we pull two books with different spines and open them side by side: THE SAME PAGE. Two titles, one book. In ours, we repeat the experiment ten times: never the same page twice. That is the definition, made visible in ten seconds. — `S05SamePage`. The pages are not staged: each is the genuine matrix product of the word on the spine, and the scene asserts that no two of the ten agree.
